ByteDance API pricing is three units and one parameter

Search “ByteDance API pricing” and you get numbers that cannot be subtracted from each other: from $0.06726/second, $5.6 per million tokens, flat $0.09/s, $0.045 per image. They describe the same family of models — Seedance for video, Seedream for images — metered three different ways. ByteDance API pricing is a unit plus a parameter, not a price list. The unit tells you what you are buying (a second of output video, one generated image, or a slice of a token budget). The parameter tells you what changes the number: for every ByteDance video model on sale at Modellix’s ByteDance provider page as of September 13, 2026, that parameter is resolution — one switch, and nothing else; the Seedream image routes are flat per image.

The short version, all live-checked today: on the Modellix route, Seedance video runs from $0.0210/second (bytedance/seedance-2.0-mini-v2v, 480p) to $0.7800/second (bytedance/seedance-2.0-t2v and -i2v, 4K), and Seedream images are $0.0350/image (5.0 Lite) or $0.0810/image (5.0 Pro). At BytePlus/ModelArk — ByteDance’s own platform — the same families are billed per million tokens for video and by pixel tier for images. That difference is not a rounding detail; it decides whether two published prices can be compared at all.

Modellix distributes these models, so this breakdown comes from a seller of the routes it describes. Where the two platforms bill in the same unit, the lower number is not always ours — you will see both directions below.

What this breakdown covers (and what it leaves out)

Before the tables, the boundary. This page covers the media models ByteDance ships under the Seedance (video) and Seedream (image) names, priced from the live model pages on September 13, 2026.

  • Included: all 17 ByteDance model pages currently on sale at Modellix — 12 Seedance video routes and 5 Seedream image routes. Model counts differ by definition, so be precise about which one you mean: 17 is the count for this provider’s on-sale pages today (the provider page’s own structured data declares numberOfItems: 17), not the platform-wide model-page count.
  • Not included — text models: ByteDance also sells Seed LLMs (Seed 1.6, Seed 2.0 Lite and friends), which bill per million tokens. That is a different product line with its own catalog, priced per million tokens on ByteDance’s own platform — not on this page. Pages that mix Seed LLM token prices into a “ByteDance API pricing” answer are answering a different question.
  • Not included — older generations: Seedance 1.x and Seedream 4.x are not on sale at Modellix as of today. Modellix carries Seedance 2.0/2.5 and Seedream 5.0. If you need a 4.x number, take it from ByteDance’s own platform — the BytePlus ModelArk pricing page lists seedream-4-0-250828 at $0.03/image (accessed September 13, 2026). We do not carry it, so we do not quote it as ours.
  • Not included — free tiers: there is no signup credit to evaluate against. The $1 registration credit ended on August 19, 2026; trial quota is now requested by email (details in the access section below).

Every price in this article was pulled from the individual model pages on September 13, 2026, and each table row links to the page it came from. Prices on this platform move; re-check before you commit a budget.

Read the table by column, not by row. Three things fall out of it:

  • The resolution spread is the budget risk, not the model choice. seedance-2.0-t2v is $0.0700/sec at 480p and $0.7800/sec at 4K — more than 11× on the same model, same prompt. Anyone budgeting from a “from $0.07/second” headline and then requesting 4K is off by an order of magnitude.
  • The cheap routes are the mini and fast tiers, and they stop at 720p. seedance-2.0-mini-v2v at $0.0210/sec is the floor of this family; it will not deliver 1080p, so if your deliverable is 1080p this price does not exist for you.
  • 2.5 costs roughly 1.5–2× the 2.0 baseline at the same tier. At 1080p: $0.5690/sec for seedance-2.5-t2v versus $0.3700/sec for seedance-2.0-t2v. You are buying longer clips (up to 30 seconds versus 4–15), mov output, and native audio generation — not more pixels.

Video-to-video is systematically cheaper than the text/image routes on the same generation: seedance-2.5-v2v starts at $0.0616/sec against $0.1030/sec for 2.5 text-to-video, and seedance-2.0-v2v undercuts 2.0 T2V at every shared tier. If your workload edits existing footage rather than generating from scratch, that gap is the single largest lever in this table. For integration specifics on the individual routes, the Seedance 2.0 API key guide and the Seedance series page cover keys and endpoints; this article stays on the money.

The parameter that actually changes the bill

Here is the finding that no comparison page states plainly: across all 17 on-sale ByteDance models there is exactly one price dimension — resolution (on the twelve video routes; the five Seedream image routes carry no dimension at all). Not duration. Not whether audio is generated. Not how many reference images you pass, up to each model’s documented maximum. The per-model pricing block literally lists one strategy key (resolution) and its values; that is what produces the ranges in the card footers.

Three billing units, one account balance: video is charged per second of output, images per generated image, and the platform’s LLM routes per token. Illustrative Modellix artwork, not a screenshot of any ByteDance product.

What that means for cost control:

  • Pick the resolution tier first, then the model. A 5-second 480p clip and a 5-second 1080p clip on the same model can differ by more than 5× — the model choice is the smaller decision.
  • Longer clips do not have a higher per-second rate. Seedance 2.5 runs to 30 seconds where 2.0 stops at 15; the published rate is per second either way. A 30-second 2.5 clip is expensive because it is 30 seconds, not because long clips are surcharged.
  • Inputs you send are priced as part of the route, not as extras. Reference images and audio inputs change what the model does, but the tables show no input-side surcharge line on this platform for these routes. What you pay is output seconds or output images.
  • Uploaded inputs are retained for about 7 days. The File API is free and API-key-authenticated (image, video and audio; default 16 MB cap) with roughly seven-day retention — long enough for a retry loop, not long enough to be your asset store. Plan re-uploads accordingly, because losing an input reference mid-batch means paying to regenerate from a new upload.
  • The model path no longer carries /async. Model invocation paths dropped the suffix on June 11, 2026; the old /async endpoints still work, so an integration written before that date will not break — but new code should call the current path. That is a migration detail, not a billing change; it is listed here because it is the one API-shape fact that shows up in every endpoint example you will copy.

Why BytePlus prices the same models differently

“Is byteplus ByteDance?” — yes. BytePlus is ByteDance’s international cloud brand, and ModelArk is its model platform, so the official route and the models discussed here come from the same company. What differs is the meter.

On the ModelArk pricing page (accessed September 13, 2026), video is billed per million tokens, with the page warning that “video pricing is determined by both the token rate and token consumption” — a higher resolution costs more because it consumes more tokens, and a video input costs more than no video input. The same page publishes per-second approximations inside its limited-time discount notices: Seedance 2.5 at 1080p at 28% off, “prices starting at approximately USD 0.41 per second” (14:00 UTC+8 August 14 through 14:00 UTC+8 September 17, 2026); Seedance 2.0 mini at 720p at 60% off, “approximately USD 0.03 per second” (August 7 through October 7, 2026); Seedance 2.0 fast at 720p at 25% off, “approximately USD 0.09 per second” (same window).

Two conclusions follow, and only two:

  1. A token price and a per-second price are not comparable without the token-consumption math. Any page that slots $5.6 / M tokens next to $0.09/second as if they were the same kind of number is comparing a rate to a product of rates. Third-party routing pages that publish a single per-second figure — OpenRouter’s Seedance 2.0 page shows “from $0.06726/second”, Atlas Cloud’s ByteDance page advertises “flat $0.09/s” — are stating their own route’s number, which is legitimate, but it is one model and one configuration, not a catalog.
  2. Official discounts are time-boxed and must be read as such. The 25–60% figures above are promotional windows with published end dates, not list prices. On the Modellix route none of the 17 ByteDance models carried a model-level discount on September 13, 2026 — so if a page claims a permanent “discounted ByteDance rate”, ask which window it is quoting.

None of this says which platform is cheaper overall, because the units do not line up well enough to support that claim. The comparable slice is the per-image one in the section above, and it splits both ways.

Reconciling the bill: logs, model discovery, and CLI checks

A price list you cannot reconcile with your invoice is a marketing page. Three platform features exist specifically so each charge can be traced back to a call.

Per-request logs with cost. GET /api/v1/logs returns paginated request logs for the account that owns the API key, each entry carrying task_id, status, model.provider, model.model_name, created_at and a cost value (Get Logs, accessed September 13, 2026). The window is capped at 30 days per request and the endpoint takes an optional end-user filter, so per-customer cost attribution is a query, not an export project. Since June 2026 the logs also record the input parameters of each call — which is what makes the resolution finding above auditable: you can see the tier you were charged for, not just the total.

Token-level detail on the LLM side. The LLM log endpoint returns prompt_tokens, completion_tokens, cached_tokens and cost per request, plus latency and tokens-per-second fields (LLM logs). Media and text routes bill from different meters, and the logs keep them separate rather than blending one balance into an unexplained number.

Discovery and schema without guesswork. GET /api/v1/models lists active models with their slugs, types, documentation URLs and a description field (List models) — the programmatic equivalent of the provider page screenshotted above. The Modellix CLI adds model batch for JSONL submissions (relevant when a 500-clip run is one command, not 500 requests), and model get-schema fetches a model’s request-body contract without an API key via the public Get Schema endpoint. Cost-wise, a schema call is free; the reason to use it before a batch is that a rejected request costs nothing but a wasted run costs your time.

Two billing rules worth writing into your own runbook: on this platform sec is defined as the duration of your output video and img as the number of output images, so the meter follows what you actually receive rather than request time (pricing units); and all three product lines — media, LLM, web tools — draw from one account balance, which returns 402 when it is short rather than silently degrading.

Top-up discounts, trial credit, and how to get a key

Three things developers ask about alongside pricing, answered from the platform’s own changelog (product updates, accessed September 13, 2026):

  • First top-up: 10% off every tier. Each top-up option carries a 10% discount on your first purchase of that tier, applied at checkout. This is an account-level discount on money you add — it is not a model discount and it does not change the per-second or per-image rates in the tables above. Keep the two in separate rows of your budget.
  • Signup credit is gone. New registrations no longer receive the $1 complimentary credit (ended August 19, 2026). Trial quota is requested by email from the support address on the site. Any guide promising a free starting balance for ByteDance models is describing a policy that no longer exists — and “is there a free ByteDance API tier” should be answered no until you have been granted trial quota.
  • One key, one balance, both directions of usage. The key you create is the credential for these media routes and for the platform’s LLM routes; webhooks (X-Webhook-URL on prediction creation) let long video jobs push results instead of polling, and the console can email you a low-balance alert at a threshold you set — which is cheaper than discovering a 402 mid-batch.

Choose direct when: you want ByteDance’s promotional windows, you need a ByteDance model we do not carry (Seedance 1.x, Seedream 4.x), you want the official platform’s support and SLA in your own cloud account, or your workload is token-shaped on ModelArk’s terms.

Choose an aggregator when: you are already calling models from several vendors and want one integration and one bill; you want per-request cost lines you can reconcile; or you are prototyping across Seedance tiers and want to switch family by changing a model slug instead of opening a second cloud account.

The thing this article does not claim is that the aggregator route is cheaper. On the one slice where both platforms bill in the same unit — Seedream image generation — the official tier is cheaper for small images and our flat rate is cheaper for large ones. Everything else is a unit mismatch that a responsible comparison refuses to paper over. If cost is your only axis, price your actual job with the table in the previous section on both routes; if integration and reconciliation are axes too, weigh them as the separate costs they are. A pricing checklist before you commit

  • Name the model and the resolution. Twelve of these seventeen routes change price with resolution; a quote without a tier is not a quote.
  • Check the unit before you compare anything. sec, img, and M tokens are not interchangeable; only same-unit figures can be placed side by side.
  • Ask whether a discount is promotional or permanent. Official windows have end dates; this platform’s ByteDance rates carried no model-level discount on September 13, 2026.
  • Keep account-level discounts separate. The 10% first top-up is money off your balance, not off the rate.
  • Budget retries, not just passes. The meter follows your output — sec of delivered video, img of delivered images — so a quality gate that rejects one in five generations still costs you the reruns you generate, and File API inputs expire after roughly seven days.
  • Read one invoice line before you scale. Pull a request log, match the cost to the model and tier you sent, and confirm the meter behaves as documented. Ten minutes now beats discovering a 4K mistake on the next thousand clips. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does ByteDance API pricing cost per video?
On the Modellix route, Seedance video runs from $0.0210/second (seedance-2.0-mini-v2v at 480p) to $0.7800/second (seedance-2.0-t2v or -i2v at 4K), all rates as of September 13, 2026. The rate is set by resolution: seedance-2.5-t2v is $0.1030/sec at 480p, $0.2310/sec at 720p and $0.5690/sec at 1080p.

What is the cheapest Seedance 2.0 API version?
Among the twelve Seedance routes on sale today, the lowest published rate is bytedance/seedance-2.0-mini-v2v at $0.0210/second at 480p ($0.0450 at 720p). It is the cheapest by design — a lightweight video-to-video tier capped at 720p. If your job is text-to-video rather than video editing, the floor is $0.0400/second (seedance-2.0-mini-t2v, 480p).

How much does Seedance 2.5 cost?
Seedance 2.5 has three routes on sale: -t2v and -i2v at $0.1030/sec (480p), $0.2310/sec (720p), $0.5690/sec (1080p), and -v2v at $0.0616/sec, $0.1380/sec and $0.3400/sec for the same three tiers. A 10-second 1080p clip on seedance-2.5-t2v is therefore $5.69. Seedance 2.5 supports clips up to 30 seconds and mp4 or mov output.

Is BytePlus ByteDance?
Yes. BytePlus is ByteDance’s international cloud brand and ModelArk is its model platform, so the official route serves the same Seedance and Seedream models described here. The difference is the meter: ModelArk bills video per million tokens and images by pixel tier, while Modellix lists a per-second rate for video and a flat per-image rate.

How much does Seedream 4.0 cost?
Not from Modellix — Seedream 4.x is not on sale here as of September 13, 2026; the Seedream line starts at 5.0 ($0.0350/image for Lite, $0.0810/image for Pro). ByteDance’s own platform lists seedream-4-0-250828 at $0.03/image on the ModelArk pricing page (accessed September 13, 2026), so that is the number to use if you need 4.0 specifically.

Is there a free ByteDance API tier?
No. The $1 signup credit ended on August 19, 2026, and there is no standing free allowance for these models. Trial quota is requested by email from the support address listed on the platform; until you have been granted it, budget from the paid rates above.

Does resolution or duration change the ByteDance API price more?
Resolution, and by a wide margin. Duration simply multiplies the per-second rate — a 30-second clip costs three times a 10-second clip at the same tier. Resolution changes the rate itself: on seedance-2.0-t2v, 4K is $0.7800/sec against $0.0700/sec at 480p, more than an 11× difference on the same model and prompt.

Why do different websites quote different ByteDance API prices?
Because they quote different units and different routes. A per-million-token figure (ModelArk’s official video billing), a per-second figure for one model under one configuration (routing platforms), and a per-image figure are three different meters — and promotional windows add a fourth variable, since official discounts of 25–60% carry published end dates. Compare only same-unit figures, and always check the date attached to a number.
