Grok 4.7: Release Date, Pricing, Benchmarks & How to Use It

September 24, 2026

Summary

Grok 4.7 is SpaceXAI's coding and knowledge-work model, released September 21, 2026. It keeps Grok 4.6's $2/$6 price, adds a larger base model, and competes with models that cost several times more on output.

Grok 4.7 is SpaceXAI’s model for coding and knowledge work, released on September 21, 2026. It replaces Grok 4.6 at the same price: $2 per million input tokens and $6 per million output tokens.

The pitch is price-performance on long tasks. SpaceXAI says it “works longer on difficult tasks” and “checks its own work more carefully,” and prices it well below GPT-6 Sol on output.

It is not a frontier leader on every chart. It trails GPT-6 Astra badly on terminal work, and its context window is half the size of the GPT-6 models. Here is where it fits.

What Is Grok 4.7?

Grok 4.7 is the current top Grok model for developers. It is available in the Grok API, Cursor, Grok Build, and through model routers.

SpaceXAI lists three changes from Grok 4.6: a new, larger base model, a longer reinforcement learning run weighted toward tasks that take hours, and native training on its Grok Bot harness.

The practical claims are that it verifies its own work more often and handles longer context better. There is also a fast variant with twice the output speed at twice the price.

The price did not change, so this is a free upgrade for anyone on 4.6. Terminal-Bench nearly doubled, which is the change most likely to show up in agent runs.

Note the effort levels. Grok 4.7 scores are mostly at xhigh, one step above 4.6’s default. Higher effort means more reasoning tokens, billed as output.

The takeaway: Grok 4.7 is cheaper than GPT-6 Sol on output-heavy work, and more expensive on long prompts. Keep prompts under 200K to stay on the base rate.

Grok 4.7 Benchmarks

SpaceXAI compares Grok 4.7 against GPT-5.6 Sol and Claude Fable 5.1, not against the GPT-6 models. We did not rerun any of these.

Grok 4.7 matches Fable 5.1 on DeepSWE and office work and trails it on CursorBench, at a fraction of Fable’s $10/$50 price. It leads clearly on the legal and electrical engineering tasks.

Vendor tables do not always agree on rival scores. OpenAI’s table puts Fable 5.1 at 67.4% on DeepSWE and 55.8% on Terminal-Bench, against 70.0% and 57.9% here, so compare within one vendor’s table, not across them.

It is weakest on long terminal sessions. GPT-6 Astra scores 57.9% on Terminal-Bench 4.0, well above Grok 4.7’s 37.6%. On GDPval, SpaceXAI’s own chart puts Grok 4.7 at 1,695 Elo, behind Fable 5.1 (1,735) but ahead of GPT-6 Astra (1,542).

What Is Grok 4.7 Good For?

  • Long coding tasks on a budget. Near-frontier DeepSWE and CursorBench scores at $6 per million output tokens.
  • Document and office work. Strong AA Briefcase and GDPval results.
  • Specialist knowledge work. Legal and engineering benchmarks are where it stands out.

It is a weaker fit for long terminal sessions, and for prompts above 200K tokens, where the price doubles.

What our team saw in a small test

We sent Grok 4.7 a faulty JavaScript median function, function median(a){a.sort();return a[a.length/2]}, with two expected results: [2, 10, 3] should return 3, and [1, 3, 5, 7] should return 4.

It found three bugs: the default string-based sort, a fractional index for odd-length arrays, and no averaging for even-length arrays. It also flagged that sort() mutates the input.

We ran its revised function locally. Both cases returned the right values, the original array stayed unchanged, and an empty array raised an error. That is one small, checkable task, not a benchmark.

How to Use Grok 4.7

The model name is grok-4.7 in the SpaceXAI API. Three practical notes:

  1. Default effort is high. Drop to low or medium for short tasks to cut reasoning tokens.
  2. No Batch API. Large offline jobs run at the standard rate.
  3. Pin the version. Aliases like ~xai/grok-latest will move to the next Grok release.
