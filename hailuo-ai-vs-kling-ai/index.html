Both vendors shipped their current flagships months ago: Kling 3.0 (released in early 2026, with native audio, multi-shot generation, and native 4K) and Hailuo 02 / 2.3 (MiniMax’s current Hailuo line, positioned around physical realism and human performance respectively).

The goal is not to crown a single winner: for motion-heavy, multi-shot, audio-synced production at API scale, Kling 3.0 is the stronger tool; for simple per-video generation, human performance, stylized character work, and low-volume casual API use, Hailuo is the better trade. Both are legitimate production choices, and most serious teams end up using both in one pipeline.

Hailuo AI vs Kling AI in 2026 means Hailuo 02 / 2.3 against Kling 3.0. Kling wins on motion and physics, multi-shot storytelling, native audio, first/last-frame control, and 4K output. Hailuo wins on human performance and stylized character work, per-video pricing simplicity, and a much lower entry price for occasional generation. The honest gap between them is narrower — and the billing difference stranger — than any consumer-facing comparison you’ll find ranked right now.

The version problem: which Hailuo and which Kling are you comparing?

Before any capability talk, the naming has to be untangled, because both vendors run multiple generations in parallel and the SERP is stuck two versions back.

Hailuo is MiniMax’s video model family. The current official pay-as-you-go page lists three members: Hailuo 2.3 Fast, Hailuo 2.3, and Hailuo 02. On Modellix’s model pages, Hailuo 2.3 is described as the flagship for human performance — character-driven storytelling, close-up emotional shots, stylized artistic videos, dialogue scenes — while Hailuo 02 is optimized for physical realism: product commercials, high-speed sports action, nature documentaries, sweeping landscapes. Hailuo 2.3 Fast is the faster, cheaper tier of 2.3. There is also a separate MiniMax H3 model billed per second (2K at $0.13/sec, 768P at $0.08/sec), which is not a Hailuo model and should not be confused with one.

Kling is Kuaishou’s video family, and its current flagship is Kling 3.0. Kling 2.5 and 2.6 are the previous generations that most ranked comparisons still benchmark. The official API billing table lists 3.0 (with and without native audio), 3.0 Turbo, 3.0 Omni (video-input variants), and the newer O1 line. If a comparison article was written before mid-2026 and talks about “Kling 2.6”, it is comparing the previous generation against Hailuo 2.3 — which is exactly the staleness pattern on the current SERP.

Capability: where each model actually wins

Motion, physics, and camera work — Kling

Kling 3.0’s core strength is physical plausibility: gravity, collisions, fabric, inertia, and consistent camera moves. This was already Kling’s advantage at 2.6 in the benchmark articles ranked for this keyword, and 3.0 extends it. fal.ai’s Kling V3 Pro vs Hailuo 2.3 Pro comparison frames Kling V3 as the model for multi-shot storytelling with per-shot prompts, custom character elements, and audio-synced video. If your content is action, product shots with real physics, or anything where how things move is the point, Kling is the safer call.

Human performance and stylized characters — Hailuo

Hailuo’s strength is people. The official Modellix model descriptions put it as the human-performance flagship: close-up emotional shots, dialogue scenes, stylized artistic video. The Reddit thread ranking first for this keyword (r/HailuoAiOfficial’s “Kling vs. Hailuo – Which One Should I Get?”) reflects the same split: users pick Hailuo for character consistency and stylized looks, and Kling for motion and realism. For talking characters, stylized animation, or content where a consistent face across shots matters more than physics, Hailuo is the better fit.

Multi-shot and storytelling — Kling

Kling 3.0 generates up to six shots in a single generation with AI-directed transitions — effectively a one-call storyboard with per-shot prompts and custom durations. Hailuo’s current Hailuo-family models don’t offer multi-shot (fal.ai lists it as not available on 2.3 Pro). For narrative sequences in one pass, Kling wins structurally; for single-scene clips, the difference doesn’t matter.

Native audio — Kling (and Hailuo’s answer is H3)

Kling 3.0’s native audio generation (Chinese and English, per fal.ai’s documentation) is a genuine feature: dialogue and sound come out of the model. Hailuo 2.3 doesn’t offer native audio generation on fal.ai’s listing. MiniMax’s answer to audio is the separate H3 model, whose official price page notes audio is free with generation — but H3 is not a Hailuo model and has different (per-second) billing. If you need synchronized audio in the model output, Kling 3.0 is the current-gen choice.

Prompt control and iteration — split

Kling 3.0 rewards structured, deliberate prompts and gives you first-frame/last-frame control — valuable for loops and scene chaining. Hailuo is generally more forgiving with loose prompts and faster to iterate on a single clip. Neither is “better” in the abstract; it depends on whether you want controlled repeatability or quick exploration.

Resolution — Kling at the top end

Kling 3.0 offers native 4K output (30 credits per second on the consumer side, per Kling’s official credit guide). Hailuo’s official price page tops out at 1080P for the Hailuo family. If delivery at 4K matters, that’s a Kling differentiator today.

The pattern behind all of this: Kling 3.0 is a motion-and-storytelling engine; Hailuo 02/2.3 is a character-and-style engine. That distinction matters more for choosing than any single benchmark.

The billing-unit trap (read this before comparing any number)

Kling and Hailuo are not priced in the same unit, and most comparisons quietly ignore this. Kling’s official API bills per second; MiniMax’s official Hailuo list bills per task (a price is tied to a resolution and duration, e.g. 768P 6s). You cannot divide Hailuo’s task price by an assumed duration and present it as an official per-second rate, because MiniMax does not publish a universal per-second conversion for Hailuo. That is also why the Modellix route pages for Hailuo display per-second ranges while MiniMax displays per-task prices: they are different billing surfaces. A like-for-like comparison needs a matched configuration — same model, same resolution, same duration — compared per finished minute. One 1080P 6-second Hailuo 02 clip is $0.49 officially; ten of them are $4.90. Kling 3.0 at 1080P with native audio is $0.168/sec, so a 10-second clip is $1.68 and a 60-second generation would be $10.08 (Kling 3.0 supports up to 15 seconds per call, so long content needs chaining or multi-shot).

Honest caveat: this is not a claim that Kling is always cheaper or Hailuo always cheaper. At 512P for short clips, Hailuo 02’s $0.10–$0.15 per 6–10s task is dramatically cheaper than any Kling tier. At 1080P, Hailuo 02 is also cheaper per finished minute: ten 6-second clips cost $4.90, versus $10.08 for 60 seconds of Kling 3.0 with native audio at $0.168/sec. Kling’s price story is not unit price — it is capability: native audio, multi-shot generation, 4K, and up to 15 seconds in a single call. The point is the shape of the comparison, which is unit- and config-dependent — and almost never stated in consumer articles. Consumer subscriptions vs API: two ways to pay

“Hailuo AI pricing” and “Kling AI pricing” are both real searches, and the honest answer has two halves.

Kling’s consumer side is credit-based. Kling’s official credit cost guide lists membership plans from $6.99/month (Standard, 660 credits, roughly 33 720p videos; promotional first-order price — renewals are $8.80/month) to $127.99/month (Ultra, 26,000 credits, roughly 1,300 720p videos), with a free Basic plan whose generated content is not for commercial use. 4K output runs 30 credits per second. These are web-app subscription prices — you are renting access to Kling’s app, not buying API calls.

MiniMax/Hailuo has no comparable consumer subscription. Hailuo is sold as API pay-as-you-go (and separately as prepaid Video Packages on MiniMax’s platform). There is no $7/month Hailuo web-app tier in the same sense as Kling’s membership; the closest consumer-ish path is the web playground on MiniMax’s platform.

The trap to avoid: comparing Kling’s subscription math against Hailuo’s API math. A “Kling costs $6.99/month vs Hailuo at $0.28 per clip” sentence looks like a comparison and isn’t one — different billing models, different commercial-use terms, different control (API vs web UI). Pick the purchase model first (app subscription or API), then compare within it. If you need programmatic access, per-second (Kling) and per-task (Hailuo) API pricing are the only fair units; if you need a web UI and don’t care about automation, subscriptions win on sticker price.

Developer experience: API shape and observability

For developers — the people actually integrating these models — the API shape matters as much as model quality.

Kling’s official API is sold through its own platform and resellers, priced in prepaid resource packages with a $0.14/unit conversion. Model IDs (kling-v3, kling-v3-turbo, kling-v3-omni) and per-second granularity are documented in the official billing table; rate limits and error semantics vary by distribution channel.

MiniMax’s official API is OpenAI-compatible at api.minimax.io/v1 (international) with a standard async submit-poll-retrieve flow for video. Hailuo tasks are priced per output specification, which makes cost prediction simple — you know the task price before you call, as long as you know the resolution and duration you’re requesting.

Both use the same async pattern: submit a job, poll or get notified, fetch the result. If you’re building a pipeline that needs both families, the practical questions are: how many vendors’ consoles do you want to hold keys for, and can you see what each task actually cost you? On that second point, Modellix’s unified API records every task’s input, output, cost, and duration on one bill — useful precisely because the two models price in different units. Published concurrency scales with funding: from 2 concurrent tasks / 100 RPM below $10 funded to 100 concurrent / 1,000 RPM at $1,000+.

The practical test for either choice: run the same prompt through Hailuo 02/2.3 and Kling 3.0 at your actual resolution and duration, and compare (a) output quality for your content, (b) cost per finished minute, and (c) failure behavior under concurrency. Both families expose async task APIs, so the test harness is nearly identical for either.

When to pick Hailuo — and when to pick Kling

Pick Hailuo (02 or 2.3) if: your content is people — character-driven storytelling, dialogue scenes, stylized artistic looks; you want a flat per-video price with no metering surprises; your workload is low-volume or occasional (a $0.10–$0.49 task price is a trivial entry cost); or you want a forgiving model for quick single-clip iteration.

Pick Kling 3.0 if: motion and physics are the core of the content (action, product motion, sports); you need multi-shot storytelling in a single generation; you want native audio in the output; first/last-frame control matters for loops and chaining; you need 4K delivery; or you generate many sub-6-second clips where Hailuo’s per-task minimum (e.g. $0.49 per 1080P 6s task) charges you for seconds you do not use — Kling’s per-second metering only bills actual seconds.

Both are legitimate production choices. The common thread across every hands-on comparison — including the creators on Reddit who actually use both — is that teams use both models in one pipeline: Hailuo for characters and style, Kling for motion and final action shots. The either/or framing is the weakest part of most articles ranking for this keyword.

FAQ

Which is better, Hailuo AI or Kling AI?

For motion, physics, multi-shot storytelling, native audio, and 4K output, Kling 3.0 is better. For human performance, stylized character work, dialogue scenes, and simple per-video pricing, Hailuo 02/2.3 is better. Neither is universally better — the honest 2026 matchup is workload-dependent, and serious teams often use both.

Is Kling AI better than Hailuo?

On the dimensions most comparison articles care about — motion fidelity, physics, multi-shot, native audio, first/last-frame control, 4K — yes, current-gen Kling 3.0 beats the current Hailuo family. On character consistency, stylized looks, and per-video price simplicity, Hailuo wins. Most ranked articles compare Kling 2.5/2.6 against Hailuo 2.3, which is a different (older) matchup than the current generation.

What is the Hailuo 02 API price?

Per MiniMax’s official pay-as-you-go page (accessed August 25, 2026): Hailuo 02 is $0.10 per 512P 6s, $0.15 per 512P 10s, $0.28 per 768P 6s, $0.56 per 768P 10s, and $0.49 per 1080P 6s. These are per-task prices; MiniMax does not publish a universal per-second rate for Hailuo.

What is the Hailuo 2.3 API price?

Per the same official page: Hailuo 2.3 is $0.28 per 768P 6s, $0.56 per 768P 10s, and $0.49 per 1080P 6s; Hailuo 2.3 Fast is $0.19 per 768P 6s, $0.32 per 768P 10s, and $0.33 per 1080P 6s. Check the live page before budgeting — prices change.

Is Hailuo AI cheaper than Kling?

It depends entirely on the config. At 512P for short clips, Hailuo 02 at $0.10–$0.15 per task is far cheaper than any Kling tier. At 1080P, Hailuo 02 is still cheaper per finished minute: ten 6-second clips cost $4.90, while 60 seconds of Kling 3.0 with native audio runs $10.08 at $0.168/sec. Kling’s premium shows up in capability (audio, multi-shot, 4K, 15-second single calls), not in unit price. The two use different billing units (per task vs per second), so only a matched-configuration comparison per finished minute is meaningful.

What are the limitations of Hailuo AI?

The current Hailuo family (02/2.3) tops out at 1080P on the official price page, offers no multi-shot generation, and doesn’t provide native audio generation the way Kling 3.0 does. MiniMax’s separate H3 model covers per-second billing and free audio, but it is not a Hailuo model.

Kling vs Hailuo — what does the community say?

The r/HailuoAiOfficial thread ranking first for “hailuo ai vs kling ai” converges on: Hailuo for character consistency and stylized looks, Kling for motion, realism, and overall physical plausibility. Multiple creators report using both in one pipeline rather than choosing.

Is there anything better than Kling AI?

For specific workloads, yes: Hailuo 02/2.3 is better at human performance and stylized characters; Veo 3.1 (Google) is widely considered better at lip-sync and cinematic dialogue; Runway is a strong third option for interface polish. A common production pattern is Kling for motion and storyboarding, Hailuo for characters, Veo for dialogue.
