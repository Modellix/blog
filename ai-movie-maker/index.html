Summary

Go from script to screen with an AI movie maker: a six-step pipeline, shot-type prompt recipes, real Seedance shots, and exactly what a trailer or a short film costs.

Every short film used to start with the same meeting: “great idea, but who is paying for the camera?” An AI movie maker skips that meeting. A script and an afternoon now produce shots that would have needed a crew.

What separates a real film from a reel of pretty clips is the pipeline: a shot list, continuity between shots, and sound. That is also where most one-click “AI movie” apps quietly cut corners.

Here is the six-step pipeline we use, the shot types that work, and what a trailer or a short actually costs.

How we tested: every clip on this page was generated by our team through the API on Seedance, at 720p unless noted. Costs are the amounts actually billed per clip.

How to Make an AI Movie in 6 Steps

  1. Write a one-line logline. If you cannot say it in one sentence, the model cannot shoot it.
  2. Turn the script into a shot list. One line per shot: framing, subject, action, camera move, light, sound.
  3. Generate every shot as its own clip. 5 to 15 seconds each; long takes up to 30 seconds on Seedance 2.5.
  4. Lock continuity. Repeat character descriptions, time of day, and light direction in every prompt.
  5. Pick keepers, then re-render. Draft at 480p, then render the approved shots at 1080p or 4K.
  6. Cut and score. Seedance generates audio in the same pass; add music and dialogue polish in your editor.

A shot-list line the model understands:

Shot 4, wide, 6 s: an astronaut in a weathered suit walks toward a crashed ship on a red desert planet under two moons. Slow dolly-in, anamorphic lens flare, subtle film grain, deep rumbling score.

Free apps

Kling’s free Basic plan and ByteDance’s Dreamina both hand out a small daily credit allowance, and both are genuinely fun for a first short film.

Kling’s own plan table is blunt about the catch: on Basic there is no watermark removal, no 1080p or 4K, and no commercial-use rights. Those arrive on the paid tiers. Dreamina likewise keeps watermark-free, campaign-ready downloads for paying members.

Free-tier catches

  • Watermark on every download. Fine for a group chat, a deal-breaker for a client or an ad account.
  • Capped resolution. Kling Basic has no 1080p or 4K, so your short film tops out below what feeds and TVs expect.
  • One clip at a time, in the slow lane. Free plans get one video per generation and no fast-track or unlimited queue. At peak hours a job can sit, stall halfway, and send you back to square one with fewer credits left.
  • A daily ration. A few clips a day will not fill a series, and a failed run eats into the same allowance.
  • No commercial rights. Anything made on the free tiers above cannot be sold, sponsored, or run as an ad.

A free AI movie maker is a fine storyboard sketchpad. It is not a production tool: watermarks and no commercial rights rule out distribution, client work, and ads.

Paid all-in-one apps

Script-to-film apps are fast for a first cut and include watermark-free, commercial output on paid tiers. The trade-off is control: you get the app’s models, its shot lengths, and its edit, and credits reset monthly whether your shoot schedule agrees or not.

Tips for generating efficiently through the API

  • Draft cheap, finish sharp. Find the shot on Seedance 2.0 Fast at 480p ($0.06/s), then re-render only the keepers at 720p or 1080p.
  • Close the tab, keep the job. Tasks run on the server. Submit, walk away, and poll the task ID later; nothing restarts because your browser did.
  • Pay for results, not errors. In our runs, failed tasks were not billed, so a rejected prompt costs a rewrite, not money.
  • Batch the shot list. Put one request per line in a JSONL file and run modellix-cli model batch to submit a whole episode at once.
  • Save outputs on completion. Result URLs expire about seven days after a task finishes, so download or copy them to your own storage right away.

Where AI Movie Makers Still Struggle

  • Take length. Most shots stop at 15 seconds; Seedance 2.5 reaches 30. Plan longer scenes as cuts.
  • Continuity drift. Faces and props can change between shots unless every prompt repeats them.
  • On-screen text. Signs and titles often come out garbled; add them in the edit.

Knowing the limits is what keeps a 3-minute short under budget.

Frequently Asked Questions About AI Movie Makers

Is there a free AI movie maker? Yes. Kling’s Basic plan and Dreamina offer daily free credits, with watermarks, lower resolution, and no commercial rights.

How much does it cost to make an AI short film? A 30-second trailer costs about $4.50 at 720p on Seedance 2.0; a 3-minute short at 1080p costs about $66.60 before retakes.

Can an AI movie maker write the script too? Some apps do. For control, write the shot list yourself and let the video model handle the images and sound.

Can I sell or submit an AI short film? Rights come with paid plans or API access, not the free tiers above. Check each festival’s AI policy too.
