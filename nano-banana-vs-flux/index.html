The short answer: they win on different jobs

Nano Banana and Flux are both production-grade image model families, but they are not interchangeable — the honest answer depends on which version you mean and what job you are doing. Nano Banana Pro (Google) wins text rendering, world knowledge, and reasoning-heavy prompts; Flux (Black Forest Labs) wins speed, cost per image, and open weights for local deployment.

A note on version mismatch, because it is the biggest source of confusion in this comparison: “Nano Banana vs Flux” without a version is not a well-defined question. Google’s family spans the original Nano Banana, Nano Banana 2, Nano Banana 2 Lite, and Nano Banana Pro; Black Forest Labs’ family spans FLUX1.1 [pro]/[ultra], FLUX.1 Kontext, and the FLUX.2 line ([pro]/[flex]/[max]/[dev]). Most comparisons online quietly pit only Nano Banana Pro against FLUX.2 — which is why the results keep flip-flopping between “wiped the floor” and “almost the same”.

This is published by Modellix, an API aggregator that carries the Nano Banana family. One honesty note up front: as of August 24, 2026, we do not carry Flux on our platform (verified against our live model list on that date), so the Flux route in this article is direct-to-BFL. The numbers below come from official pricing pages and our own price table, all accessed on August 24, 2026, and we say plainly where each side wins and loses.

The version map: which Nano Banana, which Flux

The two families as of August 2026. Each version is a distinct model with different pricing, speed, and capabilities — “Nano Banana vs Flux” without a version is not a well-defined question.

The two families as of August 2026. The versions people actually compare in production (Nano Banana Pro vs FLUX.2) are only one pair among many.

Nano Banana family (Google):

  • Nano Banana (original) — Gemini 2.5 Flash Image, released August 26, 2025. Fast and light; the model most older articles test.
  • Nano Banana 2 — Gemini 3.1 Flash Image. Adds thinking modes, web search grounding, character consistency for up to 5 people, extreme aspect ratios.
  • Nano Banana 2 Lite — Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite Image. The budget tier, priced below the original.
  • Nano Banana Pro — Gemini 3 Pro Image, released late November 2025. Highest fidelity and text accuracy; blends up to 14 reference images; the current benchmark for legible in-image text.

Flux family (Black Forest Labs):

  • FLUX1.1 [pro] — the standard text-to-image endpoint since late 2024; still one of the most-used image APIs in production.
  • FLUX1.1 [pro] Ultra — ultra-high-resolution variant of 1.1.
  • FLUX.1 Kontext — released May 29, 2025; the first widely adopted edit-by-instruction model, famous for inpainting that leaves the rest of the image untouched.
  • FLUX.2 [pro]/[flex] — released late November 2025, the current generation; up to 4MP output, up to 10 reference images, sub-10-second generation.
  • FLUX.2 [max] — the highest-quality variant of FLUX.2, with search grounding.
  • FLUX.2 [dev] — open-weight variant (non-commercial license), downloadable for local deployment.
  • FLUX.2 [klein] — the small/fast tier for real-time, high-volume work.

For the rest of this article, “Nano Banana” and “Flux” mean the families, and we name the specific version whenever a claim only holds for one of them.

Text rendering and world knowledge

This is where the consensus is clearest, and it matches the pattern across every independent test we reviewed: Nano Banana Pro is the current benchmark for text inside images. Logos, product labels, infographics, posters — Pro renders character-level correct typography more reliably than any Flux version, including across non-Latin scripts (independent tests show it winning multilingual poster tests against FLUX.2 Max on Japanese typography). FLUX.2 closed much of the gap — Black Forest Labs advertises “production-ready text” for complex typography and UI mockups, and in Vidguru’s 10-test benchmark FLUX.2 Max actually won the infographic test outright. But when the text itself is the deliverable — a poster, a label, a diagram — Nano Banana Pro is the safer pick.

World knowledge is the bigger gap. Nano Banana Pro and 2 can ground generation in Google Search, so prompts about real facts (“an infographic about the Tokyo Tower with accurate annotations”) come back correct; FLUX.2 has no live search — its world knowledge comes from training data plus a visual language model, and independent tests show it generating plausible-looking but wrong schematics where Pro returns accurate ones. If your workload involves real-world facts, precise counts, or complex scene logic (“a dog swimming will displace the water and make the waterline uneven”), that reasoning is exactly what Nano Banana’s thinking pipeline handles and what diffusion-only models still miss.

Character consistency is close to a tie. Nano Banana 2/Pro hold a consistent character identity across generations without fine-tuning (up to 5 people). FLUX.2 supports up to 10 reference images for consistent character and style — Black Forest Labs claims it is the “best character consistency available today”, and independent multi-scene tests rate both “very consistent”. For brand mascots and campaign series, either works; Nano Banana wins on prompt-only consistency, FLUX.2 wins when you have a strong reference library to feed it.

Speed, cost, and open weights

This is Flux’s territory, and the advantages are real.

Speed. FLUX.2 generates images in roughly a tenth of Nano Banana Pro’s wall-clock time in independent tests — Overchat’s comparison measured FLUX.2 at about one-tenth the time, and BFL advertises sub-10-second generation for FLUX.2 Pro at 2K. Nano Banana Pro’s reasoning pipeline is the reason it is slower, and the reason it is smarter about complex prompts. If your workflow is iterative — dozens of drafts before a keeper — that speed difference changes the economics of exploration.

Cost. At official API prices (verified August 24, 2026, full table in the next section), Flux is cheaper at every tier: FLUX1.1 [pro] at $0.04/image versus Nano Banana Pro at $0.134 per 1K–2K image is roughly a 3× gap, and FLUX.2 [klein] starts at $0.014 per megapixel. For high-volume pipelines this dominates the decision.

Open weights. FLUX.2 [dev] and the FLUX.1 [dev] weights are downloadable; you can run them locally with ComfyUI, fine-tune them, and keep data on your own hardware (the [dev] license is non-commercial — check the BFL licensing terms before building a product on it). Nano Banana has no open weights at all. This is the entire reason the “Nano Banana Pro local” search exists — if you need on-premises generation or fine-tuning, only Flux can do it.

One more honest note on the aesthetics dimension: independent tests describe FLUX.2 as aesthetic-first (painterly, cinematic, strong atmosphere) and Nano Banana Pro as logic-first. On plain photorealism both are excellent and tests largely tie — the aesthetic gap matters most for editorial and marketing imagery where Flux’s look is a feature, not a bug.

When to pick Nano Banana — and when to pick Flux

Pick Nano Banana when:

  • In-image text accuracy is non-negotiable (logos, labels, infographics, posters, multilingual typography)
  • Prompts involve real-world facts, counts, or scene logic that need reasoning, not just diffusion
  • You need character consistency across a series without a reference library
  • You are already on Google Cloud / Vertex AI and want the same contract

Pick Flux when:

  • Speed per iteration matters — FLUX.2 generates in roughly a tenth of Pro’s time
  • Cost per image at volume is the primary constraint — Flux undercuts Nano Banana at every tier
  • You need open weights for local deployment, fine-tuning, or data control (FLUX.2 [dev], non-commercial license)
  • You want the painterly/cinematic aesthetic for editorial or marketing assets
  • You have a strong reference library and want 10-image multi-reference control

And the honest middle answer: many teams run both — Flux for drafts, volume, and exploration; Nano Banana Pro for text-heavy, logic-heavy, brand-critical finals. If you need neither’s unique features, pick purely on price, and that is Flux.

FAQ

Is Nano Banana Pro better than Flux?

It depends on the job. Nano Banana Pro is better for in-image text accuracy, world knowledge, reasoning-heavy prompts, and multilingual typography. Flux (FLUX.2, or the cheaper FLUX1.1 line) is better for speed, cost per image, open weights, and the painterly aesthetic. Neither family beats the other across all dimensions — that is why online comparisons keep contradicting each other.

Which AI is better than Nano Banana?

Per dimension: Flux undercuts Nano Banana on speed and cost and offers open weights; Seedream undercuts it on cost per image and native resolution; but for text rendering and character consistency across generations, Nano Banana Pro is still the current benchmark — that consensus holds across independent comparisons and our own API testing.

Is there anything better than Nano Banana?

For legible text inside images and prompt-only character consistency, no — Nano Banana Pro is the benchmark. For cost, speed, or local deployment, yes: the Flux family beats it on those specific dimensions, as does Seedream at the low-cost end. “Better” only makes sense per dimension.

Is Nano Banana the best image generator?

Not as a blanket statement. It is the best at text rendering and world-knowledge-grounded generation; it is not the cheapest, not the fastest, and not open-source. Pick by job, not by crown.

What about Nano Banana vs Midjourney?

That is a different comparison: Midjourney is a consumer subscription product with no first-party API, aimed at creatives, while Nano Banana is an API-first model family aimed at developers. If you are building an integration, Nano Banana (or Flux) is the relevant comparison — see our Nano Banana vs Midjourney article if you need that one.
