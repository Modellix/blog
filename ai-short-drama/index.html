Summary

AI short dramas are vertical, fast, and built on cliffhangers. Here is how to make one four ways, from free apps to an API, with real Seedance scenes and the cost of a full season.

Short dramas run on three things: a vertical screen, a slap-worthy twist every ninety seconds, and a budget that would make a soap opera blush. AI now covers the first two and shrinks the third.

A scene that needed actors, a set, and a lighting crew now comes from a prompt. The catch is scale. One scene is easy; a 60-episode season with the same faces and the same tone is where the tool you pick starts to matter.

Here are four ways to make an AI short drama, what a full season costs, and why the free route stops at your personal feed.

Method 1: Free AI Drama Apps

Kling’s free Basic plan and ByteDance’s Dreamina both hand out a small daily credit allowance, and both are genuinely fun for a first drama scene.

Kling’s own plan table is blunt about the catch: on Basic there is no watermark removal, no 1080p or 4K, and no commercial-use rights. Those arrive on the paid tiers. Dreamina likewise keeps watermark-free, campaign-ready downloads for paying members.

Free-tier catches

  • Watermark on every download. Fine for a group chat, a deal-breaker for a client or an ad account.
  • Capped resolution. Kling Basic has no 1080p or 4K, so your drama tops out below what feeds and TVs expect.
  • One clip at a time, in the slow lane. Free plans get one video per generation and no fast-track or unlimited queue. At peak hours a job can sit, stall halfway, and send you back to square one with fewer credits left.
  • A daily ration. A few clips a day will not fill a series, and a failed run eats into the same allowance.
  • No commercial rights. Anything made on the free tiers above cannot be sold, sponsored, or run as an ad.

A free AI drama generator is a fun way to post a cliffhanger to your own account. It cannot carry a paid series: the watermark sits on every frame, output is capped, a handful of clips a day will not fill an episode, and the free license rules out commercial use.

Method 2: Paid Subscription Apps

Paid plans remove the watermark and allow commercial use; Kling’s Standard plan starts at $6.99 for the first month and renews at $8.80 with 660 credits.

The problem is volume. A 90-second episode is roughly eighteen shots, a season is dozens of episodes, and each shot is a manual click. Monthly credits run out long before the villain gets what they deserve.

Tips for generating efficiently on Modellix

  • Draft cheap, finish sharp. Find the shot on Seedance 2.0 Fast at 480p ($0.06/s), then re-render only the keepers at 720p or 1080p.
  • Close the tab, keep the job. Tasks run on the server. Submit, walk away, and poll the task ID later; nothing restarts because your browser did.
  • Pay for results, not errors. In our runs, failed tasks were not billed, so a rejected prompt costs a rewrite, not money.
  • Batch the shot list. Put one request per line in a JSONL file and run modellix-cli model batch to submit a whole episode at once.
  • Save outputs on completion. Result URLs expire about seven days after a task finishes, so download or copy them to your own storage right away.

Tips for Better AI Short Dramas

  • Set 9:16 every time. Vertical is the format; generate it natively instead of cropping a landscape clip.
  • Hook in the first three seconds. Open each episode on the reveal, the slap, or the line, then explain.
  • Quote the dialogue. Seedance generates audio by default, so put the exact line in quotes.
  • Keep a cast sheet. Reuse the same description for each character in every scene so faces stay put between episodes.
  • End on a push-in. A slow push-in on a shocked face is the cheapest cliffhanger in the business.
