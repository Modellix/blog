Nano Banana 2.1: Price, Benchmarks, and How It Compares to Pro

October 8, 2026
AI Image Image Generation Nano Banana
Modellix Team Official
Summary

Nano Banana 2.1 is Google's new image generation and editing model: half the per-image price of Nano Banana 2 at 1K and 2K, and ahead of Nano Banana Pro on every row of Google's model card. Here are the prices, the benchmarks, a real test, and what to run today.

Nano Banana 2.1 is Google’s new image generation and editing model, built on Gemini 3.6 Flash. Google released it on October 6, 2026 as gemini-nano-banana-2.1 and marked Nano Banana 2 as deprecated the same day.

The price is the headline: $0.0336 per 1K image, half of Nano Banana 2, and Google’s own evals put it ahead of Nano Banana Pro on every row. It is built for infographics, localized posters, and product or character sets that must stay consistent across many images.

Two catches. Input tokens cost three times more than on Nano Banana 2, and the 512px size is gone. Modellix has not added 2.1 yet, so this page also shows what to run today and how to switch in one line when it lands.

Nano Banana 2.1 Release Date and Access

Google released Nano Banana 2.1 on October 6, 2026. Its model card lists seven places to use it: the Gemini app, Google AI Studio, the Gemini API, AI Mode in Search, Google Ads, Flow, and Stitch.

In the Gemini app, free users stay on Nano Banana 2. Nano Banana 2.1 needs a Google AI Pro, Plus, or Ultra plan.

The Gemini API changelog marks gemini-3.1-flash-image (Nano Banana 2) as deprecated with no shutdown date announced, and names 2.1 as the replacement. A deprecated model can still get a shutdown date at short notice, so plan the move to 2.1 now instead of waiting for the date.

The biggest change is factual accuracy in infographics: Google’s autorater scores 2.1 nearly three times higher. That matters for anything with numbers, labels, or diagrams in the image.

Two things got more expensive. Input costs 3x more per token, which adds up on edit jobs that send many reference images. Thinking output costs 2.5x more, and thinking is on by default. Prices from the Gemini API pricing page, standard tier.

The costs the per-image price leaves out

The per-image price covers the image output tokens only. Your prompt and reference images bill as input at $1.50 per 1M tokens, and thinking bills as text output at $7.50 per 1M. Every 1,000 thinking tokens adds $0.0075 to an image, about 22% of the 1K price (our calculation).

Google’s own numbers show when thinking pays for itself: infographic factuality drops from 0.521 to 0.328 with thinking off. Set thinking to minimal for bulk thumbnails and keep it on for infographics. Even with thinking off, 2.1 still beats Nano Banana 2 on every row below.

What Can You Build with Nano Banana 2.1

Google’s model card points at four kinds of product, each tied to a row it improved most.

  • Localized infographics and posters. Diagrams, menus, and explainers with correct labels in several languages. Factuality is the biggest jump over Nano Banana 2.
  • Product catalogs. One product shot across dozens of scenes, with up to 10 objects held consistent from your references.
  • Comics and storyboards. Up to 4 recurring characters across panels; multi-character consistency gained 128 Elo over Nano Banana 2.
  • Retouching with masks and doodles. Mark an area, describe the change, keep the rest untouched.

When Nano Banana 2.1 is probably the wrong choice

  • Small text or long paragraphs. Google’s model card says small text is often blurry at 1K. Render at 2K or 4K, or set the copy in your layout tool.
  • 512px drafts or transparent backgrounds. 2.1 starts at 1K and has no transparent output. GPT Image 2.5 supports transparent backgrounds.
  • A tuned Pro pipeline that already passes review. Rerun your own prompts before switching.

How to Get Your Pipeline Ready for Nano Banana 2.1

Nano Banana on Modellix is an async task: submit, poll, then download before the result URL expires. Result URLs expire seven days after the task completes. Three habits make the 2.1 switch a one-line change:

  1. Keep the model path in config. Moving from google/nano-banana-2 to the 2.1 route should not touch your code.
  2. Stop relying on 512px. 2.1 starts at 1K, so make 1K your draft size now.
  3. Budget for input and thinking. Count reference images per job, and decide which jobs need thinking before 2.1 bills it.

Frequently Asked Questions About Nano Banana 2.1

Is Nano Banana 2.1 free?

Not in the Gemini app: free users get Nano Banana 2, and 2.1 needs a Google AI Pro, Plus, or Ultra plan. In the Gemini API it is pay per image, from $0.0336 at 1K.

Is Nano Banana 2.1 better than Nano Banana Pro?

On Google’s own model card, yes: 2.1 scores higher on all ten evals and costs a quarter of Pro at 1K. Test your own prompts before moving a production Pro pipeline.

Is Nano Banana 2 shutting down?

Google deprecated Nano Banana 2 on October 6, 2026 and has not announced a shutdown date. Google lists Nano Banana 2.1 as the replacement.

Does Nano Banana 2.1 support 4K?

Yes. It outputs 1K, 2K, and 4K, with 1K as the default. The 512px size from Nano Banana 2 is not available.
