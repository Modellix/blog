If you searched for kling ai pricing per month hoping for one number, here is the honest version: Kling AI has no single monthly price. It sells five subscription tiers priced in credits, a separate developer API priced in units, and third-party aggregators resell the same models by the second. The monthly cost you actually pay depends on resolution, native audio, and how much you generate — so the useful answer is a grid plus a way to compute your own number. This breakdown works through both, using Kling’s official pricing pages: the membership plan page and the VIDEO 3.0 credit cost guide for the consumer side, and the developer pricing page for the API side, all read on August 14, 2026.

Two things matter more than the sticker prices. First, the per-100-credit cost falls from $1.33 (Standard) to $0.62 (Ultra) — high-volume users pay less than half per credit. Second, all monthly credits expire; there is no rollover. If you buy Pro for a quiet month, the unused balance is gone. For the API side of the story — a completely separate billing system in units, not credits — see the developer section below.

The five plans, line by line: what each tier costs per month

Kling’s official credit guide (published July 28, 2026) is the most reliable current summary of the consumer plans, so the numbers below follow it rather than the many third-party summaries that quote the pre-discount “shown” prices as if they were renewal rates.

Kling’s official membership table as published in the VIDEO 3.0 credit cost guide, captured from the live page on August 14, 2026. Note the “($10 shown)”-style labels: the visible price is not the renewal price.

Free gives you 66 credits per day that expire after 24 hours. Output is capped at low resolutions, carries a watermark, and is not licensed for commercial use. It is enough to evaluate the tool, not to produce anything publishable.

Standard ($6.99 first month, $8.80 renewal) buys 660 credits: roughly 33 short 720p videos or 66 still images at standard settings, per Kling’s own plan-page estimate. It removes the watermark and unlocks commercial use, 1080p generation, fast-track queuing, and video extension. For anything resembling regular output — say, 20–30 finished clips a month with iterations — this tier is tight.

Pro ($25.99 first month, $32.56 renewal) is where the platform stops feeling restrictive: 3,000 credits (about 150 short 720p clips per Kling’s plan-page estimate), priority processing, batch generation, and early access to new features. This is the tier most serious individual creators land on.

Premier ($64.99 first month, $80.96 renewal) raises the pool to 8,000 credits with higher processing priority — the first tier that comfortably serves small teams and agencies running batch work.

Ultra ($127.99 first month, $159.99 renewal) is the volume play: 26,000 credits at the lowest per-credit cost on the consumer side. It has no annual billing option, which matters — see the annual section below.

The hidden costs that change your real monthly bill

The credit rates above are the best case. Three realities push the real monthly cost higher than the plan math suggests:

Failed generations still consume credits. On the consumer side, a prompt that trips content moderation, a server error, or an output that fails to render — the credits are spent regardless. There is no documented auto-refund policy for consumer generations. On the API side the rule is reversed: failed API tasks do not deduct units. If your prompts are experimental, this asymmetry alone can make the API route cheaper per usable clip than a subscription.

Iteration multiplies everything. The plan-vs-video math above assumes one attempt per finished clip. Real workflows take three to five attempts per usable output, so divide every “videos per month” figure by roughly three to five. A Pro plan’s 150 five-second clips at standard settings is more like 30–50 finished clips.

Credits expire monthly; add-on packs do not. Subscription credits vanish at the end of the billing cycle. Standalone credit packs — $5 for 330 credits up to $1,200 for 96,000 — carry a two-year validity and do not reset. For variable-volume work, topping up a pack is often cheaper than upgrading a tier and losing the unused monthly balance.

Annual billing and add-on credit packs: the cheaper routes

Two legitimate ways to lower the monthly number: annual billing and standalone packs.

Annual billing saves 34% on Standard, Pro, and Premier — the effective monthly rates drop to $6.60, $24.42, and $60.72 respectively. Ultra has no annual option as of August 2026, so its $159.99 renewal is the rate you pay every month. If you are choosing between Premier and Ultra, the missing annual discount is a real cost difference, not a footnote.

Standalone credit packs are separate from subscriptions and valid for two years:

Small packs cost more per credit than any subscription tier — you pay a premium for flexibility. The XL pack at $100 matches Standard’s per-credit cost exactly ($1.33 per 100 credits), and only the $1,200 Bulk pack undercuts it at $1.25 — while both avoid monthly expiry, which makes them the sensible choice for irregular output. None of the packs replace the subscription features (watermark removal, commercial license, priority queue), so treat them as a top-up, not a substitute.

The API has no monthly subscription and no monthly credit expiry — but it has a different structural catch: prepaid packages. The 180-day tier starts at $700 for 5,000 units and scales through $2,100/15,000, $3,780/30,000, $5,670/45,000, and $7,560/60,000 units — all valid 180 days with no rollover; the larger packages show a 10% discount off $4,200/$6,300/$8,400. On top of these, Kling now sells seat-based Enterprise Plans (Starter $1,550, Basic $2,950, Advanced $4,200 — credit bundles with 5–15 seats and admin controls) for teams that want a managed console rather than metered units. Package structures have been volatile across our July 26 and August 14 reads, so check the live page before committing. One clear structural advantage of the API: failed tasks do not consume units, which makes experimental workflows materially cheaper per usable clip than the consumer side.

How to choose: match your monthly volume to the cheapest route

There is no universal cheapest option, but the decision rule is short:

  • Under ~100 finished clips/month and you only need Kling — the Pro subscription ($32.56/mo renewal) is usually the right call: per-clip cost is low, priority queue helps iteration, and the first-month discount lowers the entry cost. Watch the credit expiry if your output is bursty.
  • Variable volume, or a one-off campaign — skip the subscription entirely: buy a $100 add-on pack (valid two years) or, if you are a developer, use Kling’s API trial ($9.80) and then a pay-as-you-go route. You pay a small per-credit premium and never lose a balance to expiry.
  • You need Kling plus other models, or you want no prepaid minimum — an aggregator like Modellix removes the package math and the expiry risk entirely; on identical tiers its metered rate sits below Kling’s list price, and you can test with free credit before adding payment.
  • Sustained high volume on Kling alone — Ultra at $159.99/mo renewal, or Kling’s larger API packages once you will reliably consume the units inside 180 days. At that scale the per-unit discount beats the alternatives.

Is a Kling subscription worth it? For a creator producing publishable, watermark-free video monthly, yes at Pro and above — but budget for iteration and the native-audio multiplier before choosing a tier, because the plan math above is best case. For programmatic or experimental work, the API or an aggregator route is usually the cheaper answer. FAQ

How much does Kling AI cost per month?
Kling AI’s consumer plans run $6.99–$127.99 for the first month and $8.80–$159.99 at renewal (Standard $8.80, Pro $32.56, Premier $80.96, Ultra $159.99), each with a monthly credit allowance. The real monthly cost depends on resolution, native audio, and iteration rate — a 5-second 1080p clip costs roughly 40–60 credits depending on audio.

Is Kling AI free?
There is a free tier: 66 credits per day that expire after 24 hours. It produces watermarked, non-commercial, low-resolution output — enough to test the tool, not to produce publishable work.

How to get Kling AI credits for free?
The free tier gives 66 daily credits. Beyond that, Kling occasionally runs first-subscription promotions (reflected in the first-month prices above), and some reseller platforms offer free trial credits on the same Kling models — Modellix, for example, grants free credit for evaluation.

Do Kling AI credits roll over?
No. Monthly subscription credits expire at the end of each billing cycle. Standalone add-on credit packs are valid for two years and do not expire monthly.

How much does Kling 3.0 cost?
Kling 3.0’s API rate is $0.084/s at 720p and $0.112/s at 1080p without audio, rising to $0.42/s at 4K; native audio adds roughly 50% at lower resolutions. On the consumer side, VIDEO 3.0 consumes 6–12 credits per second depending on resolution and audio. For a full grid, see our Kling 3.0 cost breakdown.

Is a Kling AI subscription worth it?
For regular, publishable video output, Pro and above are competitive on a per-clip basis — but only if you consume most of the monthly credits. Bursty or experimental workloads pay for credits they lose to expiry; in those cases add-on packs or pay-as-you-go API routes cost less in practice.

Which Kling plan is the cheapest per credit?
Ultra at $0.62 per 100 credits is the cheapest consumer tier, more than twice as efficient as Standard’s $1.33. If you do not need 26,000 credits a month, Pro’s $1.09 is the best value per credit among the mid tiers.
