Summary

AI short dramas are vertical, fast, and built on cliffhangers. See why the format exploded, how a 60-second episode is structured, and what a full season costs, with real Seedance scenes.

Short dramas run on three things: a vertical screen, a twist every minute, and a budget that would make a soap opera blush. AI now handles the first two and shrinks the third.

One scene is easy. A season is not: the same faces for 60 episodes, a hook in the first three seconds of each one, and a cost per episode that still leaves room for profit.

Here is why the format took off, how a 60-second episode is built, and what a season costs.

How we tested: every clip on this page was generated by our team through the API on Seedance, at 720p unless noted. Costs are the amounts actually billed per clip. All scenes are native 9:16.

Why AI Short Dramas Took Off

The numbers explain the gold rush. According to MIT Technology Review:

  • An average of 470 AI-generated short dramas were released every day in January, according to DataEye.
  • AI can cut the roughly $200,000 cost of a North American short drama by 80% to 90%.
  • Production that took three to four months can now take less than a month.
  • The global micro-drama market reached $11 billion in 2025 and is expected to hit $14 billion by the end of 2026.

Twelve shots, one beat, one cliffhanger. Open on the twist, explain it later; viewers decide to swipe in the first three seconds.

Write a Production Brief the Model Understands

For each scene, give the model six things: location, character action, camera angle, emotion, the line of dialogue in quotes, and where the scene ends.

Vertical short-drama scene, 9:16, cinematic realism. Location: a modern glass office at night. Action: a woman in a black blazer slides a signed contract across the desk. Camera: tense close-ups. Emotion: cold triumph. Dialogue: "You are fired, and so is your plan." Ending: the man freezes.

Keep a cast sheet and paste each character’s description into every scene, so the heroine still looks like herself in episode 40.

Free apps

Kling’s free Basic plan and ByteDance’s Dreamina both hand out a small daily credit allowance, and both are genuinely fun for a first drama.

Kling’s own plan table is blunt about the catch: on Basic there is no watermark removal, no 1080p or 4K, and no commercial-use rights. Those arrive on the paid tiers. Dreamina likewise keeps watermark-free, campaign-ready downloads for paying members.

Free-tier catches

  • Watermark on every download. Fine for a group chat, a deal-breaker for a client or an ad account.
  • Capped resolution. Kling Basic has no 1080p or 4K, so your drama tops out below what feeds and TVs expect.
  • One clip at a time, in the slow lane. Free plans get one video per generation and no fast-track or unlimited queue. At peak hours a job can sit, stall halfway, and send you back to square one with fewer credits left.
  • A daily ration. A few clips a day will not fill a series, and a failed run eats into the same allowance.
  • No commercial rights. Anything made on the free tiers above cannot be sold, sponsored, or run as an ad.

Fine for posting one cliffhanger to your own account. A series needs hundreds of shots, no watermark, and commercial rights, and the free tiers offer none of the three.

Paid apps

Paid plans remove the watermark and allow commercial use (Kling’s Standard plan starts at $6.99, then $8.80 a month). The problem is volume: a season is hundreds of manual generations, and monthly credits run out long before the villain gets what they deserve.

Tips for generating efficiently through the API

  • Draft cheap, finish sharp. Find the shot on Seedance 2.0 Fast at 480p ($0.06/s), then re-render only the keepers at 720p or 1080p.
  • Close the tab, keep the job. Tasks run on the server. Submit, walk away, and poll the task ID later; nothing restarts because your browser did.
  • Pay for results, not errors. In our runs, failed tasks were not billed, so a rejected prompt costs a rewrite, not money.
  • Batch the shot list. Put one request per line in a JSONL file and run modellix-cli model batch to submit a whole episode at once.
  • Save outputs on completion. Result URLs expire about seven days after a task finishes, so download or copy them to your own storage right away.
