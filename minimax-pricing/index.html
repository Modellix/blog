The two MiniMaxs — and why “minimax pricing” isn’t one answer

Search for “minimax pricing” and every result on the first page assumes you’re asking about MiniMax’s LLM models — M3, M2.5, token plans, per-million-token rates. That’s a real and valid question, but it’s not the one this article is answering.

MiniMax sells two separate product lines with completely different pricing models. One is the LLM line (M3, M2.5, coding models), billed per token through pay-as-you-go keys or monthly Token Plans at $20–$120/month. The other is the media generation line — video via Hailuo models, image generation, and music — which MiniMax prices through an entirely different system: prepaid “video points” packages starting at $1,000 per month.

If you got here looking for LLM pricing, MiniMax’s own pay-as-you-go page and OpenRouter’s MiniMax M3 page have the per-token rates you need. This article is for developers and teams who need MiniMax’s video and image models behind an API — the Hailuo line — and want to understand what they actually cost across different channels.

One thing you should know up front: we run Modellix, an aggregator that offers MiniMax’s Hailuo models with pay-as-you-go pricing. We have a commercial interest in this comparison. Every MiniMax figure here was read from MiniMax’s own pricing docs on July 28, 2026, and every Modellix figure from our own model pages on the same date. Where we couldn’t verify something, we say so.

Why official pricing is more expensive — and when it still makes sense

The per-second numbers above look competitive for MiniMax direct. But the $1,000 minimum changes the math for most teams. The Standard package gives you 3,760 points. At Hailuo 2.3’s 768p deduction rate of 1 point per 6 seconds, that’s 22,560 seconds of video — about 376 minutes, or just over 6 hours of finished output. To use that up in 30 days, you need to generate roughly 12.5 minutes of video every single day.

If your actual usage is a tenth of that, you haven’t paid $0.0443 per second. You’ve paid $1,000 for about 37 minutes of video, and your real cost per second is $0.45 — ten times the list rate. The unused points don’t roll over. They’re gone.

MiniMax direct makes financial sense in exactly one scenario: you reliably consume video generation at high, predictable volumes — enough to fill the package every month, and ideally enough to move up to the Scale or Business tiers, where the effective per-second rate drops further (at Scale, Hailuo 2.3 at 768p works out to about $0.0397/s). At that level, the package discount genuinely beats aggregator pricing.

For everyone else — prototypes, early-stage products, variable workloads, teams evaluating whether Hailuo fits their pipeline — the $1,000 commitment turns MiniMax’s own API into the expensive option. This is the opposite of what you’d expect from buying direct, and it’s the single fact that’s missing from every currently-ranking pricing page.

There’s also a data residency consideration worth flagging. MiniMax is a Chinese company, and calling its API — whether directly or through an aggregator — sends your requests to infrastructure in China (MiniMax’s docs carry a Chinese ICP license). If your organization can’t send user data to Chinese infrastructure, Hailuo is off the table at any price. Resolve that before you compare per-second rates.

What about MiniMax image and music generation?

If you’re only here for video, the image and music pricing is worth a glance because it flips the narrative in both directions.

Image generation. Modellix’s cheapest image model — across all 12 suppliers and 200+ models — is MiniMax’s image-01, at $0.0040 per image. That’s cheaper than any Kling, Alibaba, or Google image model on the platform. MiniMax image-01 supports text-to-image at up to 1440×2560, with a prompt enhancement feature that improves output quality at no extra cost. For context, Kling’s cheapest image model (V1) runs $0.0070/img — nearly double — and Nano Banana starts at $0.0080/img. So if your primary use case is image generation at volume, MiniMax on a pay-as-you-go aggregator is the budget option that nobody is writing about.

MiniMax’s official API docs don’t publish a standalone image generation pricing page — it sits within the broader API ecosystem, and its direct pricing isn’t broken out in a way that lets you build a per-image cost estimate without an account. What matters for cost planning: MiniMax offers at least three image models (image-01, image-01-fast, and an image-edit variant), each with different quality/speed tradeoffs, and the aggregator pricing reflects those tiers.

Music and audio. MiniMax’s Music-3.0 and Music-2.6 models generate audio from text prompts. On the aggregator side, Music-3.0 costs $0.0400 per generation and Music-2.6 costs $0.0200. MiniMax’s own audio subscription starts at $9.90/month with usage-based overage, but the specifics depend on generation length and quality settings that aren’t documented without a login. The free tier that does exist — two specific endpoints on Music-3.0 and 2.6 — is covered in our MiniMax free API guide.

Comparing MiniMax’s media pricing against the other Chinese AI API providers tells a useful story. Kling — which most people benchmark MiniMax against for video — starts at $700 for its cheapest production package (5,000 units, 180-day validity). MiniMax’s video entry is $1,000, but Kling’s package lasts six months while MiniMax’s expires in one. Kling’s cheapest per-second rate for 3.0 at 720p is $0.084/s; MiniMax’s Hailuo 02 at 512p is $0.013/s — six times cheaper, but at a resolution that Kling doesn’t go down to. The useful comparison is Hailuo 2.3 at 1080p ($0.089/s) against Kling 3.0 at 1080p ($0.112/s): MiniMax is about 20% cheaper on list price, but the one-month expiry erases that advantage for low-volume users. This is the structural problem with prepaid packages: the per-second comparison that looks favorable on a spreadsheet disappears the moment you don’t fill the package.

FAQ

Is MiniMax cheaper through an aggregator or directly?

Per second of output, MiniMax’s official list rates are lower. At the Standard $1,000 package, Hailuo 2.3 at 768p works out to $0.044/s — versus $0.054/s through a pay-as-you-go aggregator. But the effective cost is never just the list rate: it’s the list rate multiplied by the package size you committed to, divided by however much of it you actually used. If you use 10% of the package, your real cost is ten times the list rate. For low-to-medium volume, the aggregator’s higher list rate works out cheaper in practice.

Can I try MiniMax video generation for free?

MiniMax’s official API doesn’t offer a free tier for video — the cheapest path is the $1,000 Standard package. Modellix provides a free $10–$30 credit for new users, no credit card required, which lets you test every Hailuo model without committing. You can also use the free Music-3.0 and Music-2.6 endpoints for audio generation without paying.

What’s the difference between Hailuo 02 and Hailuo 2.3?

Hailuo 02 is the previous-generation model, optimized for speed and cost. Hailuo 2.3 is the current flagship with better motion quality, longer clips (up to 10s vs 6s on some tiers), and higher resolution support. The pricing reflects this: Hailuo 02 at 512p is the cheapest MiniMax video endpoint at $0.013/s, while Hailuo 2.3 at 1080p is the most expensive at $0.089/s on official pricing. If your application prioritizes cost over quality — drafts, thumbnails, internal testing — Hailuo 02 is the route. If output quality drives your product, Hailuo 2.3 is where the investment goes.

Do unused MiniMax video points roll over?

No. Points expire at the end of the package validity period — 30 days for Standard through Business tiers. There is no rollover, no extension, and no refund for unused points. This is the single most important fact about MiniMax’s official pricing, and it’s buried in a tooltip on the pricing page. The 30-day clock means you’re not buying video generation capacity — you’re buying a monthly consumption quota that resets to zero whether you used it or not.

How does MiniMax pricing compare to Kling?

For the flagship models at 1080p: MiniMax Hailuo 2.3 is $0.089/s, Kling 3.0 is $0.112/s — MiniMax is about 20% cheaper on list price. But Kling’s Standard package ($700 for 5,000 units) lasts 180 days versus MiniMax’s 30, and Kling’s catalog is broader (roughly 40 endpoints versus MiniMax’s 15 on an aggregator). The structural issue is the same for both: prepaid packages punish variable usage. If you’re choosing between them, the expiry window — not the per-second rate — is the number that should drive the decision.

How to choose: a practical decision framework

The decision reduces to volume, with two clear thresholds and one override.

If your monthly video generation is below roughly 6 hours of finished output, MiniMax’s own API is the expensive option — not because the per-second list rate is higher, but because the $1,000 package will expire with unused points. A pay-as-you-go aggregator gives you the same Hailuo models with no commitment. You can test with a free $10–$30 Modellix credit — no credit card required — and start paying only when you’re ready to scale. If you’re exploring MiniMax’s free access options first, our MiniMax free API guide walks through what’s available without paying. Our MiniMax model collection has all 15 endpoints with live pricing and a playground to test before you write code.

If your monthly volume consistently fills the Standard package or beyond, and you’re comfortable with the 30-day expiry, MiniMax direct starts to make financial sense. At the Scale tier ($4,500 for 18,900 points), the effective per-second cost for Hailuo 2.3 at 768p drops to roughly $0.040/s — below the aggregator’s lower-tier pricing. And the Business tier opens up custom pricing with no RPM cap, which aggregators can’t match.

The override is data residency. If your organization prohibits sending data to Chinese infrastructure, MiniMax is unavailable through any channel. The alternative is a Western-hosted video model — Veo 3.1 (Google, US-hosted through Google Cloud) or Runway (US-based) — and that comparison is a different article.

If you need to route between multiple model families — Hailuo for one type of shot, Veo for another, something cheap for drafts — then per-model pricing stops being the deciding factor. A unified API with one key and one invoice becomes the dominant concern. That’s the scenario aggregation is built for, and it’s also the scenario where we have a commercial interest in your answer.
