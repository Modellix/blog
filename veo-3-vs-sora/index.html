Video quality: photorealism vs prompt adherence

On raw output quality, the two models genuinely split — and pretending one beats the other outright is how you get a dishonest comparison.

Sora 2 wins single-shot photorealism. CNET’s head-to-head (November 2025) found Sora’s ballerina noticeably more lifelike, and Android Authority’s test (October 2025) reached a similar conclusion, noting that Sora 2 “showcases realistic physics without any of the errors exhibited in Veo 3’s result.” If your use case is a single hero shot where realism is everything, Sora 2 is the stronger generator.

Veo 3.1 wins prompt adherence, camera work, and multi-shot consistency. Tom’s Guide’s seven-prompt audio test (November 2025) gave Veo 3.1 the win on consistent prompt adherence and audio complexity, while noting Sora 2 “often looked better.” The practical difference shows up in structured scenes: reviewers across tests report Veo holds character identity across shots and follows camera-movement instructions more faithfully — the difference between a usable clip and a reshoot.

Illustrative comparison of the two quality profiles — photorealism on the left, prompt adherence and multi-shot consistency on the right. Concept artwork, not a screenshot of either platform.

Native audio: both have it — the difference is control

This generation of video models made audio a default, not an add-on. Veo 3 introduced synchronized audio at Google I/O 2025 — a first for a major model — and Sora 2 matched it with synchronized dialogue and sound effects at launch. CNET’s review describes the feature as “game-changing” when Google shipped it, and OpenAI’s own Sora 2 announcement centers the same capability.

Where they differ is control and reliability. Tom’s Guide’s audio-focused test series scored Veo 3.1 higher on audio complexity and synchronization consistency across seven prompts (a 5–1 win); CNET, by contrast, gave the edge to Sora on audio. Neither model is reliably studio-grade — lip sync can still slip on complex multi-speaker scenes — so if your pipeline needs generated dialogue that stays synced, the third-party evidence leans Veo, though the reviewers split on which model sounds better.

Access and longevity: what still exists in September

The consumer-facing picture is the part most comparisons get wrong, because it changed while they weren’t looking.

Sora is no longer an available consumer or API option. CNET’s November 2025 review described the former consumer tier and its output characteristics; the app closed on April 26, 2026, and the API’s published shutdown date was September 24, 2026. Treat old access instructions as obsolete.

Veo is reachable through four routes. The Gemini app with a Google AI Pro plan (~$20/month, per CNET’s review) is the consumer path; Google AI Studio offers a limited free tier for experiments; and for production there is Vertex AI or the Gemini API — or aggregators that resell the same models. CNET’s review notes Veo consumer clips are fixed at 8 seconds landscape, while developers get adjustable aspect ratios through the API.

For a production decision, “which app do I open” is the wrong question. The right question is: which API will still answer requests next year? Today, that’s Veo 3.1 — and every aggregator carrying it (Modellix included) inherits that longevity, plus whatever the reseller’s own pricing and limits are.

Developer experience: API shape and integration

Both models follow the same modern pattern — submit an async job, poll or receive a webhook, fetch the result — so integration shape is not a differentiator. The video generation guide for Sora and Google’s Veo API both expose per-second billing, resolution parameters, and audio output as first-class fields.

The practical differences are operational:

  • Billing units. The archived Sora price list billed per second, while Veo’s current API prices vary by model and output resolution. Compare a live Veo quote against your historical Sora usage records, not against an available Sora plan.
  • SDK and ecosystem. OpenAI’s API is one endpoint family (image, video, audio, realtime) under one key; Google’s Veo lives alongside Gemini in Vertex and the Gemini API. Neither is objectively simpler — it depends on what else you already use.
  • Availability. Sora’s published shutdown date has passed. Existing teams should inventory failed calls and migrate their submit, polling, webhook, and result-handling logic to a live provider.

For Veo specifically, our Veo 3.1 API guide with code walks through the submit–poll–retrieve lifecycle, error codes, and webhook delivery on the Modellix side.

Where Sora 2 remains a useful benchmark

Sora 2 is no longer a live API choice. Its former strengths still matter when evaluating archived outputs or deciding what a replacement must preserve:

  • Photorealism. CNET and Android Authority found Sora strong on single-shot realism and physical motion.
  • Audio and scene continuity. Prior reviews describe synchronized dialogue and sound effects; use these as criteria when testing replacement models.
  • Historical cost analysis. The old $0.10/sec entry price can help explain past spend, but it is not a current purchase option.

For current video work, choose among live routes and test the output against the characteristics your former Sora workflow depended on. Modellix’s OpenAI models page shows the live catalog; it does not include Sora video.

When to choose Veo 3.1 for current work

Veo 3.1 remains available for new production work:

  • Longevity. No sunset date, three access routes, and an active release cadence — Veo 3.1 arrived 5 months after Veo 3, and Google keeps shipping (see the Veo 3.1 announcement for the October 2025 feature set).
  • Prompt adherence and audio sync — the two axes where third-party testing leans Veo (5–1 in Tom’s Guide’s audio test; CNET gave Sora the edge on audio).
  • Resolution headroom — 4K on the standard tier, versus 1080p max on Sora’s pro tier.
  • Distribution — Vertex, Gemini API, AI Studio, and aggregators like Modellix — see our Google models on Modellix page — which list Veo 3, Veo 3.1, Veo 3.1 Fast, and Veo 3.1 Lite with transparent per-second pricing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Sora being discontinued?

OpenAI’s published API shutdown date was September 24, 2026, and this guide treats the API as discontinued. The web and app experiences closed April 26, 2026. The OpenAI discontinuation notice explains content export and deletion.

Is Sora 2 better than Veo 3?

Depends on the axis. Historical reviews favored Sora 2 for some single-shot realism tests and Veo 3.1 for prompt adherence and audio sync. For a new integration, availability matters too: Sora’s published API shutdown date was September 24, 2026, while Veo remains available.

How much does Sora 2 API cost vs Veo 3?

OpenAI listed Sora 2 at $0.10/sec at 720p and Sora 2 Pro at $0.30–$0.70/sec up to 1080p in the August 18, 2026 snapshot; that API is no longer treated as available. Veo 3.1’s August snapshot listed $0.40/sec at 720p/1080p and $0.60/sec at 4K, with Fast and Lite tiers; check Google’s live pricing before budgeting.

Can I use Veo 3 for free?

Google AI Studio offers a limited free tier for experimentation, and the Gemini app’s paid plans include Veo usage with generation limits (CNET’s review notes hitting a cap after five videos on the entry plan). Production use is paid via Vertex AI or the Gemini API.

Which is better for image-to-video, Veo 3 or Sora 2?

Third-party reviews favor Veo for image-to-video consistency and camera control. Sora 2’s image-to-video output can serve as a historical quality benchmark, but its API is no longer an available option.

What happens to my Sora videos after the shutdown?

OpenAI says content can be exported via sora.chatgpt.com/sunset, and that after the final export window closes, data associated with Sora use will be permanently deleted. Export sooner rather than later.

Does Modellix offer Sora?

No. Modellix carries 210+ image and video models — including Veo 3, Veo 3.1, Veo 3.1 Fast, and Veo 3.1 Lite — but Sora is not currently in the catalog. We state that boundary rather than blur it: if you need Sora, call OpenAI directly.

What are good Veo 3 alternatives to Sora?

Kling, Seedance 2.0, and Wan 2.x are the other production-grade options in 2026, all available through Modellix and other aggregators. The Seedance vs Kling matchup is covered in its own comparison article.
